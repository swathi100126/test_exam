## Note 1
Title: What are life processes
Topic: Nutrition
Section: 1.1
Content: Life processes are the basic activities that living things carry out to stay alive. They give energy, help growth and repair the body. The main ones are nutrition, respiration, transportation and excretion.

## Note 2
Title: Photosynthesis
Topic: Nutrition
Section: 1.2
Content: Plants make their own food by photosynthesis. It happens in the chloroplasts of leaves, where chlorophyll traps sunlight. Carbon dioxide and water are used to make glucose, and oxygen is released. Equation: 6CO2 + 6H2O -> C6H12O6 + 6O2. Common mistake: plants also respire, not only photosynthesize.

## Note 3
Title: Nutrition in humans
Topic: Nutrition
Section: 1.3
Content: Food is broken down in steps: mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, then large intestine. Saliva starts breaking down starch in the mouth. The stomach digests proteins, and the small intestine finishes digestion and absorbs nutrients into the blood. The liver makes bile, which helps digest fats. Undigested food leaves through the large intestine.

## Note 4
Title: Aerobic respiration
Topic: Respiration
Section: 1.4
Content: Aerobic respiration breaks down glucose using oxygen to release energy. It starts in the cytoplasm and finishes in the mitochondria. Equation: C6H12O6 + 6O2 -> 6CO2 + 6H2O + energy. The energy is stored as ATP, which cells use for work.

## Note 5
Title: Anaerobic respiration
Topic: Respiration
Section: 1.5
Content: Anaerobic respiration breaks down glucose without oxygen and gives less energy. In yeast, the products are ethanol and carbon dioxide. In our muscles during heavy exercise, the product is lactic acid, which can cause cramps. Comparison: aerobic gives much more energy than anaerobic.

## Note 6
Title: Breathing vs respiration
Topic: Respiration
Section: 1.6
Content: Breathing is taking in oxygen and giving out carbon dioxide. It is a physical process. Respiration is the chemical process inside cells that releases energy from glucose. Common mistake: saying breathing and respiration are the same thing. Breathing supplies the oxygen that cell respiration needs.

## Note 7
Title: Human heart and double circulation
Topic: Transportation
Section: 1.7
Content: The human heart has four chambers: two atria on top and two ventricles below. The right side pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs, and the left side pumps oxygenated blood to the body. Blood passes through the heart twice in one full cycle, so this is called double circulation. Valves stop blood from flowing backwards.

## Note 8
Title: Blood and blood vessels
Topic: Transportation
Section: 1.8
Content: Blood carries oxygen, food, hormones and waste around the body. It has plasma, red blood cells (carry oxygen with haemoglobin), white blood cells (fight germs) and platelets (clot blood). Arteries carry blood away from the heart and have thick walls. Veins bring blood back to the heart and have valves. Capillaries are very thin and let materials pass to the cells.

## Note 9
Title: Transport in plants
Topic: Transportation
Section: 1.9
Content: Xylem carries water and minerals from the roots up to the leaves. Phloem carries food made in the leaves to all parts of the plant. This movement of food is called translocation. Common mistake: mixing up the two. Remember: xylem = water, phloem = food.

## Note 10
Title: Transpiration
Topic: Transportation
Section: 1.10
Content: Transpiration is the loss of water as vapour from the leaves, mainly through stomata. It pulls water upward through the xylem and helps cool the plant. It is faster in hot, dry and windy weather. Example: a plant loses more water on a sunny afternoon than on a cool, humid morning.

## Note 11
Title: Excretion in humans
Topic: Excretion
Section: 1.11
Content: Excretion is the removal of waste products made by the body. In humans the kidneys filter blood and make urine. The nephron is the filtering unit of the kidney. Urine passes through the ureters to the bladder, and leaves the body through the urethra. Sweating through the skin and breathing out carbon dioxide are also forms of excretion.

## Note 12
Title: Common mistakes
Topic: Mistakes
Section: 1.12
Content: Do not mix up breathing (physical) with respiration (chemical, in cells). Remember xylem carries water and phloem carries food. Plants respire all the time, not only photosynthesize. Aerobic respiration needs oxygen, anaerobic does not. Arteries carry blood away from the heart, veins carry it back.
